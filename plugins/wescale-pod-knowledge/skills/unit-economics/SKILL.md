# Unit economics

Read `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/knowledge/concepts/unit-economics.md` and `sources/5-4-profit-is-a-choice.md`. Product setup detail is in `sources/3-1-launch-your-products.md`.

## The reference stack — a $29.99 tee

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Price (S-XL) | $29.99, +$1 per size step |
| Production, 1 print location | ~$7.91 |
| Shipping | ~$4.75 |
| Payment processing | 2.9% + $0.30 (~$0.90) |
| Profit before ads | $16-20 |
| Profit after paid traffic | $8-12 |

Cost of goods runs 45-50% at the start; the target is to pull it to **40-42%**. Above 44-45% the model stops working.

## ROAS is a margin statement

2.0 → ~0% · 2.5 → ~10% · 3.0 → ~18% · 4.0 → ~25-30%. Breakeven around 1.9.

This is the most clarifying number in the whole course: the gate to *scaling* is the gate to *breaking even*, not to making money.

## P&L targets (% of revenue)

Cost of goods 40-42% · ad spend 25-33% · overhead 2-3% · refunds ~1%.

Overhead is the line that creeps — one cited case let software reach ~10% of revenue when ~$5K/month would have covered top-tier Shopify and Klaviyo (claimed).

## Caveats to state

Costs are Printify US rates as of recording and drift. The worked P&L in lesson 5.4 does not fully reconcile on camera — the source card flags which figures to distrust. Bookkeeper around $100K/month.
