# The WeScale method — full synthesis

One system, five stages, taught across 12 hours. This page is the whole method in one place.
Every number here traces to a lesson card in [`../sources/`](../sources/); figures the instructor
asserts without shown evidence are marked `(claimed)`.

## The shape of the thing

The course is not "how to make a t-shirt store." It is a testing machine with four gates:

```
BRAND ──► DESIGN ──► STORE ──► MOCKUP TEST ──► DESIGN TEST ──► CATALOG SCALING ──► CRO ──► SCALE
                                  gate 1          gate 2            gate 3          gate 4
                              find the image   find the designs   get CPC under $1   ROAS 2.0+
                              that gets clicks  people want        across catalog    for 2 days
```

You do not advance until the gate opens. Most of the course's specific numbers are gate criteria.

## Stage 1 — Brand

Goal: a live store and a reason to exist, in about 30 minutes.

1. Spin up Shopify + Printify through BuildMyPOD rather than configuring manually — the course's
   claim is minutes instead of 2-4 weeks and $500-2,000 of setup ([1.1](../sources/1-1-launch-your-store-in-minutes.md)).
2. Pick a niche with self-deprecating, identity-shaped humour. The buyer is purchasing a badge of
   who they are, not a garment.
3. Name + logo in ~5 minutes, 1-2 iteration rounds, no more ([1.2](../sources/1-2-create-your-brand.md)).
4. Write the brand why. This is the lesson the instructor claims took years to learn: people don't
   buy the shirt ([1.3](../sources/1-3-brand-why.md)).

Trap: perfectionism at this stage. Nothing here is load-bearing until designs get tested.

## Stage 2 — Design

Goal: 100 designs. Not 10.

- The volume claim is the backbone of the whole course: a survey of 10,000+ sellers is cited as
  showing 52% of them run 250-500 live designs, and everyone past 250 designs had crossed $10K
  in sales `(claimed)`. Winner rate is 5-10% of designs ([2.2](../sources/2-2-create-winning-designs.md), [2.3](../sources/2-3-scale-to-100-designs.md)).
- Research first: 30-50 proven designs per niche, capped at a 2-hour timer. Save 10-15 to an
  inspiration bank; saving 100+ causes paralysis ([2.1](../sources/2-1-design-research.md)).
- Production specs: 5000x5000px export for print DPI, 1-3 colours per design.
- Mockups: 1080x1080, 1:1, ~10 variations, max 2-3 supporting props ([2.4](../sources/2-4-create-mockups-that-sell.md)).

See [[design-volume]].

## Stage 3 — Store

Goal: products live, priced for a machine that must survive paid traffic.

The price ladder and the cost stack are the two things to get exactly right — see
[[unit-economics]] for the full arithmetic. In short: a $29.99 tee costs about $7.91 to produce
and $4.75 to ship, leaving $16-20 before ads and $8-12 after ([3.1](../sources/3-1-launch-your-products.md)).

- 6-8 colours per design converts best (tested against 3 and against 14).
- Conversion benchmark: 3-4% good, 1.5% bad.
- Free shipping over $74.99, flat ~$4.87 below it — the threshold is an AOV lever, not a courtesy
  ([3.3](../sources/3-3-business-setup.md)).
- Keep the theme plain. The instructor attributes the "keep it simple" rulings to $1.26M of tested
  ad spend `(claimed)` ([3.2](../sources/3-2-brand-your-store.md)).

## Stage 4 — Marketing

This is the core of the course. Three tests in sequence, each with its own gate.

### Gate 1 — the mockup test

You are testing the *image*, not the design. Which photo makes a stranger click?

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Budget | $12.50/day, ~$50 per round |
| Window | Thursday morning → Sunday 11:55pm |
| Ads per round | 5, identical except the mockup |
| Objective | Sales, optimise for Purchase, open/Advantage+ audience, US, 18+ |
| Minimum sample | ≥5 link clicks before judging |
| Winner | under $1.00 cost per link click |

Roughly 3 rounds to find a winner; only ~10-30% win on round one `(claimed)`. When you find the
winner, reskin the whole catalog to that mockup style — about 4-5 hours for ~100 products
([4.4](../sources/4-4-launch-mockup-test.md), [4.5](../sources/4-5-analyze-mockup-test.md)).

Do not touch a running test. No pausing, no copy edits, no mid-flight budget changes.

### Gate 2 — the design test

Same $12.50/day, but now the winning mockup carries ~100 designs. You are looking for 2-3 winning
designs out of 100 — explicitly not profit yet. Winner bar loosens to under $1 CPC with 3-5 link
clicks, because each design gets far less budget ([4.6](../sources/4-6-launch-design-test.md), [4.7](../sources/4-7-scaling-your-catalog.md)).

### Gate 3 — catalog scaling

Add 25 designs a week. Roughly 1 winner per 20-30 designs tested `(claimed)`. The mechanism the
course sells: catalog size drags CPC down. The flagship case claims $5.00 CPC in January falling to
$0.37 at 2.3 ROAS as the catalog went 100 → 150 → 200 designs `(claimed)`.

Expect 2-4 weekly cycles to get catalog-wide CPC under $1.

### Gate 4 — conversion

Only once traffic is cheap do you fix the store. Targets: add-to-cart 6-8%, reach-checkout 5-6%,
sessions-converted 2.5-3%, AOV above $45 ([4.8](../sources/4-8-turn-traffic-into-sales.md)).

Diagnose in this order — the worst metric relative to target is the one you fix, and you fix only
one at a time. See [[conversion-diagnosis]].

The gate to scaling: ROAS 2.0+ sustained two consecutive days. Never raise budget before that.
Typical catalog size when this first happens: 150-250 designs.

## Stage 5 — Scale

### Email is the margin

Email is where $1-in/$2-back becomes $1-in/$3-4-back. Target 25-30% of revenue from email, split
roughly 50/50 flows vs campaigns ([5.1](../sources/5-1-email-marketing.md), [5.5](../sources/5-5-your-scaling-roadmap.md)).

Popup: 8-second delay, 10-15% discount. Start campaigns at 20 subscribers, 1/week alternating
content and sales, working up to 2-3/week. Sunset anyone who hasn't opened in 120 days.

The flagship brand's own numbers: flows $87K vs campaigns $45K — flows are two thirds of email
revenue `(claimed)`. Note that brand did ~$400K in sales before setting up email at all, which is
the cautionary part of the story, not the impressive part.

See [[email-flows]].

### Ad scaling bands

Read trailing-7-day ROAS, adjust every 2-3 days. Breakeven sits at about 1.9.

| Trailing 7-day ROAS | Action |
|---|---|
| ≥ 2.5 | budget +20% |
| 2.2 – 2.4 | budget +15% |
| 1.8 – 2.2 | hold |
| 1.6 – 1.8 | budget −15% |
| < 1.6 | budget −20% |

Keep feeding it: 5-10 new ads/week, ~15 live in the ad set at this budget level. Static-ad hit rate
is about 1 winner per 20 ads ([5.3](../sources/5-3-scaling-your-ads.md), [5.5](../sources/5-5-your-scaling-roadmap.md)).

### Profit is a choice

The P&L discipline the course claims moves margin from 3-5% to 20%+ `(claimed)`:

| Line | Target as % of revenue |
|---|---|
| Cost of goods | 40-42% (44-45%+ is trouble) |
| Ad spend | 25-33% |
| Overhead | 2-3% |
| Refunds | ~1% |

ROAS maps to margin roughly: 2.0 → ~0%, 2.5 → ~10%, 3.0 → ~18%, 4.0 → ~25-30%. That mapping is the
single most useful thing in stage 5 — it tells you that scaling at 2.0 ROAS is scaling at zero
profit ([5.4](../sources/5-4-profit-is-a-choice.md)).

Bring in a bookkeeper around $100K/month. Don't look at Google Ads below $30-50K/month.

## The steady state

Once the machine runs: 25-50 new designs/week, ≥2 email campaigns/week, CPC $0.50-0.75, CR ≥2.5%,
AOV ≥$45, overall ROAS 2.3-3.0, email 25-30% of revenue.

## What to be skeptical about

- Revenue figures are the instructors' own, unaudited, and frequently framed as typical when they
  describe the top of the distribution.
- Several case-study numbers don't reconcile on camera — the source cards flag each one rather than
  smoothing it over. Notably the $115K/$47K ad-spend pair in 4.1 and the P&L gross-sales figure in 5.4.
- The "52% of 10,000 sellers run 250-500 designs" survey is the load-bearing claim for the entire
  volume strategy, and no methodology is shown.
- Business-setup content (LLC, tax) is US-specific.

The method's internal logic is sound regardless: cheap clicks first, conversion second, budget last.
The gates are the product; the revenue screenshots are the marketing.
