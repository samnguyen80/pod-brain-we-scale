# Unit economics of a $29.99 tee

Every threshold in the course descends from this arithmetic. If the margin structure is wrong, no
amount of ad optimisation saves the business.

## The stack

| Line | Amount | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Price | $29.99 | S-XL; +$1 per size step (2XL $30.99, 3XL $31.99) |
| Production | ~$7.91 | one print location, Printify |
| Second print location | +~$4.00 | rarely worth it early |
| Shipping | ~$4.75 | standard, ~5 day delivery |
| Payment processing | ~$0.90 | 2.9% + $0.30 |
| **Profit before ads** | **$16-20** | |
| **Profit after paid traffic** | **$8-12** | |

Cost of goods runs ~45-50% of price when starting out, even with Printify Premium. The stage-5
target is to pull that to 40-42%; above 44-45% the model stops working.

## Why $45 AOV is the real target

A single $29.99 shirt leaves $8-12 after ads. That is a business that cannot absorb a bad week.
Two-item orders change the arithmetic entirely, because the second item carries no new acquisition
cost — the ad already paid for the customer.

This is why the course pushes AOV above $45 rather than pushing price above $29.99. Raising price
fights conversion; raising basket size doesn't.

## The shipping threshold is an AOV lever

Free shipping over $74.99, flat ~$4.87 below it. The threshold is set deliberately above a
two-shirt order to pull toward three.

Watch the Printify default: per-item shipping escalates sharply on 2+ items (~$4.77 → ~$7.15-7.30+).
Left unfixed it silently taxes exactly the multi-item orders the whole strategy is trying to create.

## ROAS is a margin statement

The single most clarifying mapping in the course:

| ROAS | Net margin |
|---|---|
| 2.0 | ~0% |
| 2.5 | ~10% |
| 3.0 | ~18% |
| 4.0 | ~25-30% |

Breakeven sits around 1.9. This means the gate to *scaling* (2.0 ROAS) is the gate to breaking even
— not the gate to making money. Scaling at 2.0 buys growth at zero profit, which is a legitimate
choice but should be a conscious one.

## P&L targets at steady state

| Line | % of revenue |
|---|---|
| Cost of goods | 40-42% |
| Ad spend | 25-33% |
| Overhead | 2-3% |
| Refunds | ~1% |

The course claims this discipline moves margin from 3-5% to 20%+ `(claimed)`. Overhead is the line
that creeps — one cited cautionary case let software reach ~10% of revenue (~$35K/month) when
~$5K/month would have covered top-tier Shopify and Klaviyo `(claimed)`.

## Caveats

Costs are Printify US rates as of the recording and drift. The worked P&L in lesson 5.4 does not
fully reconcile on camera — the source card flags which figures to distrust.
