# 3.1 Launch Your Products

Stage: 3) Store
Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hduC-C8pBpE&t=8620s
Transcript: [../../transcripts/3-1-launch-your-products.md](../../transcripts/3-1-launch-your-products.md)

## The job of this lesson
A merchant should walk away able to fully configure Printify's store/account settings once, then upload a product end-to-end (shirt selection, colors, design placement, pricing, publish) and repeat that workflow fast enough to get all 100 designs live on Shopify within about a week.

## Playbook
1. In Printify, rename the store to your brand name under Store settings. `[02:26:04]`
2. Fill in business info under Account → My Account → Name of Business (use LLC name if you have one, else brand name) plus your name/contact/address, then click Submit. `[02:26:30]`
3. Set Ship From Address — use Printify's default rather than your personal address (also available for UK/Germany if selling international). Remember to click Save on every screen. `[02:27:24]`
4. Configure Order Settings: enable order rerouting with a cap around $2 extra cost; choose "exact product matches only" (not similar); keep Printify Choice as supplier so it picks the cheapest/closest printer. `[02:27:24]`
5. Set order submission to automatic at a fixed time — recommended 11:55pm daily — so orders push to production right before day-end and cost-of-goods is easy to reconcile against your card statement. Personalized orders: manual (unless you use personalization). Delayed orders: manually send to production. Keep "receive daily emails" on to catch order issues. `[02:28:58]`
6. Set publishing preference to General Profile (shipping profile fix happens later on Shopify). `[02:30:55]`
7. Activate Printify Premium using discount code "wescale" for one month free (then $29/mo, cheaper yearly) — cuts product cost up to 20%. `[02:31:55]`
8. In Shopify, bulk-select any pre-existing "Build My Pod" placeholder products, tag them "BMP products," and set them to Draft — this avoids confusing them with your real Printify uploads. `[02:34:03]`
9. Pick your t-shirt blank from only 3 options: Gildan 64000 (default/best value), Bella Canvas 3001, or Comfort Colors 1717. Avoid unknown/cheap mystery brands. `[02:36:40]`
10. In Printify's product designer, select 6-8 colors total: always include black, white, navy, then 3-4 more that match your niche/branding. Use "make a specific design for [color]" to swap a light-colored version of the design onto dark shirts (and vice versa) so the print pops on every color. `[02:40:35]`
11. Build the design asset in Canva: increase canvas size (helps hit 300+ DPI on Printify), enable transparent background, download, then drag-and-drop into Printify. `[02:44:40]`
12. Position the design roughly where a v-neck/collar line would sit — a few inches below the chin/neckline, not centered on the torso and not down near the belt. Check with Printify's Preview before saving. `[02:45:51]`
13. Deselect any color where the design doesn't visually pop (e.g., dark design on charcoal) rather than uploading a bad-looking variant. `[02:49:31]`
14. Leave "Global fulfillment" off if only targeting the US — turning it on limits which colors you can offer. `[02:49:31]`
15. On the pricing screen, ignore Printify's suggested retail price; set your own per-size pricing manually (or fix later via Shopify bulk edit). `[02:53:26]`
16. Set the price ladder by size, incrementing roughly $1 per size step up (e.g., S-XL one price, 2XL +$1, 3XL +$2). `[02:55:00]`
17. Choose Standard shipping (~5 days) as the default over Economy or Expedited. `[02:58:23]`
18. Click Publish in Printify, then verify the product shows Active in Shopify. `[03:01:29]`
19. Speed-hack for scaling to 100 products: duplicate an already-configured product in Printify (copies all settings/prices/colors), then just swap the design file, recheck color fit, rename the title, and republish — this cuts per-product time to roughly 1 minute once you're warmed up. `[03:07:55]`
20. Work in batches (design a batch, then upload a batch) rather than perfecting one product at a time — the instructor found batching faster personally, but says either approach is fine; find your own rhythm. `[03:02:07]`
21. Use the 80/20 upload schedule: Day 1 upload 20 products, Day 2 another 20, Days 3-5 the remaining 60 — all 100 live by the end of one week. `[03:12:52]`
22. Back in Shopify, for each product connect the "Color" swatch metafield (Category = T-shirts → Variants → connect metafield → Color) so swatches show actual color names/shades instead of generic Printify labels — adjust color shades if they render oddly (e.g., charcoal too dark). `[03:17:34]`

## Numbers and thresholds
- Return customer rate example cited as a good result: 22.8% `[02:24:10]` (claimed)
- Cost of goods typically ~45-50% of price when starting out, even with Printify Premium `[02:31:55]`
- Printify Premium: up to 20% off product cost, $29/month after 1 free month via "wescale" code (yearly plan cheaper) `[02:32:29]`
- Target conversion rate: 3-4% is good; 1.5% is bad `[02:41:09]`
- Recommended color count per design: 6-8 (tested against 3 and against 14 — 6-8 converted best) `[02:41:37]`
- T-shirt price ladder: S-XL $29.99, 2XL $30.99, 3XL $31.99 (add ~$1 per size step) `[02:55:00]`
- Production cost per shirt (1 print location): ~$7.91 `[02:56:58]`
- Adding a 2nd print location costs an extra ~$4 from Printify `[02:56:58]`
- Shipping cost: ~$4.75 (standard) `[02:56:58]`
- Shopify payment processing: 2.9% + $0.30 per order (~$0.90 on a $30 order) `[02:57:24]`
- Profit before ads: ~$16-20 per organic sale `[02:57:24]`
- Profit after paid traffic cost: ~$8-12 per shirt `[02:57:56]`
- Standard shipping delivery time: ~5 days; Economy is about $1 cheaper but slower `[02:58:23]`
- Suggested upload pace once warmed up: ~1 minute per product (5 products in ~5 minutes) `[03:12:52]`

## Tools and services named
- Printify — print-on-demand catalog, order fulfillment, product designer
- Printify Premium — subscription for discounted product costs, discount code "wescale" for 1 month free, $29/mo after
- Shopify — storefront, product catalog, tagging/draft workflow, bulk product editor (mentioned, detailed in 3.3)
- Canva — design canvas resize, background removal/transparent export for print files
- Gildan 64000, Bella Canvas 3001, Comfort Colors 1717 — the only 3 recommended t-shirt blanks

## Watch-outs
- Don't offer too many colors (14+) — it causes decision paralysis and tanks conversion; the instructor's own first store had this problem. `[02:41:09]`
- Don't upload a design on a color where it doesn't visually pop even if the mockup looked fine — customers will be unhappy if the live product looks worse than expected. `[02:52:13]`
- Avoid low-resolution designs (Printify flags "medium/low resolution"); target at least 300 DPI by upsizing the Canva canvas before export. `[02:44:10]`
- Printing on 2+ locations (front+back) adds real cost (~$4 extra) — don't do this on early/untested products; save it for once a design is a proven winner. `[02:56:58]`
- Personalization settings are explicitly not something the instructor has tested — if using it, research independently rather than following her defaults. `[02:29:56]`
- The "out of stock" bug seen when previewing early products is a known Shopify-Printify sync quirk, addressed in the next lesson via shipping profile settings, not something to panic-fix immediately. `[03:04:29]`
