# 4.1 How to Drive Traffic

Stage: 4) Marketing
Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hduC-C8pBpE&t=17529s
Transcript: [../../transcripts/4-1-how-to-drive-traffic.md](../../transcripts/4-1-how-to-drive-traffic.md)

## The job of this lesson

A merchant should walk away understanding WHY Meta (Facebook + Instagram) is the recommended single traffic channel to start with, how the "customer-financed" ad flywheel is supposed to work mechanically, and the three-phase testing philosophy (mockup → design → store optimization) that structures every ad decision from here forward. This lesson is conceptual framing, not a click-by-click setup — that starts in 4.2.

## Playbook

1. [04:53:57] Pick ONE traffic channel to start: Meta (Facebook/Instagram). The lesson argues you only need one channel to scale to $1M/year, and Meta is positioned as the best starting platform because ad costs have stayed flat-to-declining since Jan 2021 despite inflation (claimed).
2. [04:55:15] Mental model — the "flywheel": ad spend converts to sales, sales minus COGS (Printify fulfillment) and overhead leaves excess profit, and that excess profit is reinvested into the next round of ad spend. Shopify payouts land in 1-2 days, so the reinvestment loop can restart almost immediately.
3. [04:59:33] Decide your testing budget BEFORE starting: pick a dollar amount you are fully willing to lose and a time window you'll commit to it, and mentally frame it as "money invested in your own learning," not money withdrawn from savings.
4. [05:01:20] Adopt the three-phase testing strategy and only ever change one variable at a time:
   - Phase 1 — Mockup test: same design, same ad copy, same audience, vary only the mockup image.
   - Phase 2 — Design test: once mockup is locked in, vary which of your ~100 designs perform best.
   - Phase 3 — Store optimization: once designs are validated, tweak the storefront to increase how much each buyer purchases.
5. [05:05:14] Do not attempt to run any ad yet — Facebook Business Manager / ad account / page / pixel infrastructure must be built first (covered in 4.2) before any campaign can launch.

## Numbers and thresholds

- Starting budget recommendation: **$50 per weekend** until profitable, then ramp up. [04:59:33] (claimed)
- Minimum viable starting capital to launch a brand: "a couple hundred bucks to a few thousand max." [04:58:11] (claimed)
- Flywheel example math: $100 spend → $300 sales → $50 excess profit after COGS/overhead → reinvest $150 spend → $450 sales → $75 profit → reinvest $225 spend → $600-700 sales → $113 profit → next reinvestment ~$260. [04:55:40]-[04:57:09] (claimed, illustrative — not Slice Club's real numbers)
- Sloth Hiking Club case study monthly revenue: Jan ~$900 → Feb ~$3,300 → Mar ~$7,000 → Apr ~$17,000 → May ~$50,000 → Jun ~$100,000 (2,000+ orders / ~4,000 shirts). [04:57:09]-[04:58:11] (claimed)
- "From $115,000 in sales, Sloth spent about $47,000 on ads." [04:58:11] — **ambiguous**: unclear whether this $115K/$47K pair is the June figure alone, a cumulative first-half total, or a separate reference point; not reconcilable with the monthly figures as stated. Do not treat this ROAS ratio as verified.
- Sloth's first-year total: grew from $0 to $800,000 in sales, with ~$300,000 spent on ads to generate that revenue (per intro framing). [04:52:13] (claimed)

## Decision rules

None explicit in this lesson — it is framing/mindset only. Concrete kill/winner rules begin in [[analyze-mockup-test]].

## Tools and services named

- Meta Ads Manager / Facebook + Instagram — the single recommended paid ad channel
- Printify — order fulfillment, cost of goods sold in the flywheel math
- Shopify — payout timing (funds in 1-2 days) enables the reinvestment loop
- (Explicitly tested but NOT recommended as a starting channel: Google Ads, TikTok, LinkedIn, YouTube, X)

## Watch-outs

- Do not read the flywheel example dollar amounts as a guaranteed or typical ROAS — explicitly framed as "results are not typical" and illustrative only.
- Do not conflate the $115K/$47K Sloth figure with the monthly progression — the transcript does not clarify the time window it covers; treat as unverified.
- Nerves before launching a first campaign are framed as normal, not a signal something is wrong.
- Success is framed as depending on ability to follow a system and read data, not creative or media-buying talent — set expectations accordingly before the team invests in "ad creative skill."
