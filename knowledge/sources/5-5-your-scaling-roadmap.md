# 5.5 Your Scaling Roadmap

Stage: 5) Scaling
Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hduC-C8pBpE&t=40061s
Transcript: [../../transcripts/5-5-your-scaling-roadmap.md](../../transcripts/5-5-your-scaling-roadmap.md)

## The job of this lesson
A merchant should be able to run a weekly "scorecard" that diagnoses the single highest-leverage metric holding back profit, apply the full budget bump/cut decision tree by ROAS band, and execute one repeatable 9-step weekly workflow (designs → new-arrivals email → static ads → comment engagement) that ties every prior lesson into one system.

## Playbook

**1. Weekly scorecard fields** `[11:08:37]`–`[11:11:03]`: total sales, net profit margin (from the P&L), ad spend + Facebook ROAS, blended/MER ROAS (= total sales ÷ total ad spend), CPC, conversion rate, AOV, email stats (flow revenue, campaign revenue, popup signup rate), cost of goods % of revenue, overhead % of revenue, and the "inputs" row: new designs launched, new ad creatives tested, email campaigns sent. Run and peer-reviewed weekly in the $50K+/month "mastermind" cohort, fully open-book `[11:09:06]`–`[11:09:32]`.

**2. Diagnostic sequence when margin is below target** — always start with ROAS, not "how are the ads doing":
- Check overall (blended) ROAS against this margin map: 2.0 ROAS → ~breakeven margin (ad spend = 50% of revenue); 2.5 → ~10% margin (±20% variance, i.e. 8-12% acceptable); 3.0 → ~18% margin; 4.0 → ~25-30% margin `[11:11:57]`–`[11:13:07]`.
- If actual margin is well below what that ROAS predicts, there's a "leaky bucket" — the only two other places it can hide are cost of goods sold and overhead `[11:13:40]`–`[11:14:38]`.
  - Cost of goods target: 40-42% max (44-45%+ is tough). Levers: base pricing ($29.99 for a Gildan 64000 tee, higher for Comfort Colors/Bella Canvas), discount depth capped at 10-15% via the popup, and AOV ≥ $45 — a higher AOV dilutes cost of goods because each additional item in an order ships more cheaply than the first (~$5 to ship the first item vs. ~$2.50 for the second) `[11:14:38]`–`[11:16:12]`. Printify's volume rebate further lowers this as the brand scales `[11:16:12]`–`[11:16:39]`.
  - Overhead target: 2-3% of revenue once past ~$10K/month (below that, Shopify alone can eat ~5%, so the target doesn't fully apply yet). At $10K/month, 3% = $300: roughly Shopify $39-49, Klaviyo $50-100, ~$100 left for misc apps. If overhead runs 5-7%+, audit and cut subscriptions `[11:16:39]`–`[11:18:04]`.
- If margin *does* line up with the ROAS map (no leak) → the lever is raising ROAS itself, worked in strict priority order `[11:18:04]`–`[11:21:38]`:
  1. **CPC** — target $0.50-0.75 overall. If it's above $1, the fix is simply more/new designs `[11:18:56]`–`[11:19:52]`.
  2. **Conversion rate** — target ≥ 2.5%. If below, work the funnel top-down: add-to-cart rate → collection/product page → reach-checkout → sessions-converted `[11:19:52]`–`[11:20:22]`.
  3. **AOV** — target ≥ $45. If low, check shipping-rate settings first, confirm a $75+ free-shipping threshold, remove distracting announcement-bar offers, and surface related products `[11:20:22]`–`[11:20:44]`.
  - Once all three are in range, overall ROAS should land at least 2.0, ideally 2.3-3.0 — don't over-push AOV/ROAS past that; there's a point of diminishing returns where pushing volume is the better lever `[11:20:44]`–`[11:21:38]`.

**3. Email as % of revenue** `[11:21:38]`–`[11:22:34]`, `[11:36:24]`–`[11:37:24]`: target 25-30% of total revenue, split roughly 50/50 between flows and campaigns. Popup signup-rate target 5-10% (this brand runs 12-13%). Klaviyo is the starting platform; once past $10K/month the instructor recommends moving to a paid tool referred to (phonetically) as "Aaliyah" — spelling unverified, confirm the actual product name before acting on it. If email is under 20% of revenue, prioritize flows (welcome, abandoned cart, post-purchase) and send 2-3 campaigns/week. Once popup + flows + campaigns compound to ~30%, the ad account becomes far more resilient to ROAS dips because email revenue pads the down days.

**4. Store conversion rate** target: 2-3%, ideally 3%+ `[11:38:46]`.

**5. Budget bump/cut rules**, evaluated every 2-3 days (skip Mondays/Tuesdays — traffic patterns are still normalizing right after the weekend) `[11:31:32]`–`[11:32:06]`, `[11:40:18]`, using trailing-7-day ROAS per campaign/ad set:
- < 1.6 → **cut 20%**
- 1.6–1.8 → **cut 15%**
- 1.8–2.2 → **hold**
- 2.2–2.4 → **bump 15%**
- ≥ 2.5 → **bump 20%**
`[11:33:33]`–`[11:34:30]` (breakeven ≈ 1.9-2.0 depending on cost of goods). This table refines 5.3's looser version by adding the <1.6 and 1.8-2.2 bands. Also check yesterday's ROAS against the trailing-7-day number — if they disagree (e.g., a strong week but a rough last day), hold rather than bump into an apparent downturn `[11:34:01]`–`[11:35:00]`.

**6. Account-level ROAS gate**, layered on top of the per-campaign rule `[11:35:00]`–`[11:35:57]`: overall account ROAS < 1.8 → stop bumping anything, launch new ad creatives instead; 1.8-2.0 → scale cautiously while still testing new creatives; ≥ 2.5 → in a great spot, keep running the protocol as-is.

**7. Cohort-stage margin targets** `[11:40:18]`–`[11:41:12]`: under $5K/month → expect roughly breakeven; $5K/month ("test" cohort) → 5-10% net margin; $10K+/month ("scale" cohort) → 10-15%; $50K+/month ("mastermind" cohort) → 15-25%. Prerequisites before scaling budget aggressively at the $10K+ stage: ≥25 designs/week (raised from an earlier 20/week bar), P&L run daily, flows live, ≥1-2 campaigns/week `[11:30:36]`–`[11:31:32]`. Below $10K, it's still fine to push daily budgets well past $15-20/day — even to $50-100/day — *if* there's a genuinely consistent design pipeline ready to test; just don't jump the gun without that pipeline `[11:30:10]`–`[11:31:01]`.

**8. The weekly 9-step workflow** `[11:42:39]`–`[11:56:24]`:
1. **Make new designs** — 25-50/week. Reference scale: this brand runs 1,200-1,300 total live designs, Sloth runs ~355; a beginner should expect needing 600-700 designs for comparable results `[11:43:06]`–`[11:43:34]` (claimed).
2. **Send a "new arrivals" campaign email** — no discount code, so full margin is kept. Subject line = design count + a niche-relevant emoji; body = a short, personal, brand-voiced note from the founder + a link to the collection. These are among the highest-performing, zero-discount emails in the whole program `[11:44:02]`–`[11:46:39]`.
3. **Route clicks to a free "new arrivals" collection** — reuse the existing collection URL sorted "Date: new to old" instead of building a separate collection `[11:47:53]`–`[11:48:48]`.
4. **Identify winners** via Shopify's product-sales breakdown — which new designs actually sold off the email traffic `[11:49:18]`–`[11:49:44]`.
5. **Turn those winners into static ads** — same process as 5.2/5.3, now with a third signal source (email sales, alongside catalog CPC and catalog sales); run Thursday-Sunday `[11:49:44]`–`[11:50:10]`.
6. **Engage in the ad's comment section** — reply/react (GIFs are fine), treat product-idea comments as free research, and where feasible build the requested design same-day with AI tools and reply with the live link. Total loop cost: roughly $50-75 in ad spend; these email-validated ads should outperform average tests since they've already proven they can sell `[11:50:10]`–`[11:52:46]`.
7. **Repeat weekly** — at least 2 emails/week total (the new-arrivals send plus a periodic discount/offer send, timed to holidays where relevant) `[11:52:46]`–`[11:53:45]`.

## Decision rules
- If margin doesn't match the ROAS→margin map → check cost of goods (>42%?) then overhead (>3%?) before touching anything else.
- If cost of goods is too high → review pricing, discount depth, and AOV (higher AOV dilutes per-order shipping cost).
- If overhead is 5-7%+ → run a subscription audit and cut.
- If CPC > $1 (or just high relative to your own account) → launch more/better designs — described as "almost always" the fix.
- If conversion rate < 2.5% → work the funnel top to bottom: add-to-cart → product/collection page → reach checkout → sessions converted.
- If AOV < $45 → check the shipping-rate table, confirm the $75+ free-shipping threshold, remove announcement-bar distractions, add related products.
- If a campaign/ad set's trailing-7-day ROAS is ≥2.5 → bump +20%; 2.2-2.4 → +15%; 1.8-2.2 → hold; 1.6-1.8 → -15%; <1.6 → -20%.
- If trailing-7-day ROAS and yesterday's ROAS disagree → hold; wait a day to confirm the trend before bumping.
- If overall account ROAS < 1.8 → stop bumping any budgets, launch new creatives instead.
- If overall account ROAS is 1.8-2.0 → scale cautiously while still testing new creatives.
- If email revenue is < 20% of total → prioritize flows and 2-3 campaigns/week until it climbs toward 30%.
- If under $10K/month but the design pipeline is genuinely consistent → it's fine to run daily budgets well above $15-20/day before formally hitting the $10K gate.
- If stuck at any sales plateau → don't touch structure, targeting, or ad copy; the fix is always more and better designs.

## Watch-outs
- "Aaliyah" is a phonetic guess at the tool name from the transcript — verify the actual product before relying on it.
- A diagnosis like "ROAS needs to go up" is a formula, not an action — always translate it down to a concrete lever (e.g., CPC too high → launch new designs) `[11:24:26]`–`[11:25:00]`.
- The instructor frames the pull toward account-structure/targeting/ad-copy tweaks as a brain-wiring bias toward novelty and instant gratification — naming it is offered as the main defense against chasing shiny objects instead of design volume `[11:26:48]`–`[11:28:47]`.
- All benchmark numbers are this instructor's own experience/community averages, explicitly framed as "not financial advice" and "your results will vary" `[11:25:46]`–`[11:26:14]`.
