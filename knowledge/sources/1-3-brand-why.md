# 1.3 Brand Why

Stage: 1) Branding
Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hduC-C8pBpE&t=2433s
Transcript: [../../transcripts/1-3-brand-why.md](../../transcripts/1-3-brand-why.md)

## The job of this lesson
A merchant should walk away with a written mission statement / brand "why" (Simon Sinek's Start With Why framework applied via a Claude prompt) saved into a persistent Claude Project for the brand, and understand that this mission — not the product — is what drives repeat purchases and word of mouth.

## Playbook
1. Understand the underlying theory: per Simon Sinek's "Start with Why," people buy why you do something, not what you sell — illustrated with a "we sell coffee" shop vs. a shop selling a belief ("mornings should feel like possibilities"); the why turns a transaction into a relationship. `[00:41:27]`
2. Compare the two outcomes directly: a niche-only brand (their "Seasonal Collections" demo) stayed "surface level," while adding a real mission/why (Sloth Hiking Club) drove repeat purchases across new drops, social shares, email list growth, and word-of-mouth referrals. `[00:42:44]`
3. Open Claude (claude.ai, free account works) instead of ChatGPT for this step — the instructor states Claude is better at writing/creative thinking, which this task needs. `[00:45:33]`
4. Use this exact prompt structure: "Use a Simon Sinek Start with Why process to come up with a mission statement, product line roadmap, and marketing angles for [brand name]," followed by a short brand description (niche + tone + who it serves). `[00:45:33]`
5. Review the output: it will include a core belief, mission statement, product line ideas, and marketing angle ideas — but only take the mission statement/why from this step; the rest (product ideas, marketing angles) gets used later in the course. `[00:47:41]`
6. Create a Claude Project named after your brand, then add this chat to the project — this builds a persistent knowledge base so every future Claude conversation about the brand already has the why, strategy, and mission loaded as context. `[00:47:41]`
7. Spend just 5 minutes generating your own brand's why using this same prompt — don't overthink it; the niche/name/logo are still not locked in at this stage either. `[00:48:37]`
8. Self-test the output: read it out loud and ask "does this feel true to me / do I connect with this / does it make me feel something?" If yes, it's good enough — it doesn't need to be polished prose, one paragraph is enough, and it can be refined later. `[00:49:00]`

## Numbers and thresholds
- Time to generate a brand why via this method: about 30 minutes total for the full foundation (niche + logo + why), vs. what the instructor says would cost "thousands of dollars from an agency" `[00:50:21]` (claimed)
- Time to draft just the why itself: ~5 minutes `[00:48:37]`
- Instructor's personal timeline: took 5-6 years of doing print-on-demand before understanding customers weren't buying the t-shirt itself `[00:49:26]` (claimed)
- Instructor's recent track record: past 8 brands did $1M+ in sales in their first year — after 10 earlier brands that were "complete flops" `[00:51:13]` (claimed)
- Stated revenue impact of a strong brand why: can be "the difference between $3,000 versus $30,000 per month in sales" `[00:49:26]` (claimed, illustrative — not a measured study)

## Tools and services named
- Claude (claude.ai) — used specifically for the Start With Why prompt (mission, product roadmap, marketing angles) — free account sufficient; Projects feature used to persist brand context
- (Reference, not a tool) Simon Sinek's "Start with Why" book/TED talk — cited as the conceptual framework being applied

## Watch-outs
- Don't skip straight to product/marketing ideas from the Claude output in this lesson — only the mission/why is meant to be finalized here; the rest is intentionally deferred to later lessons. `[00:47:41]`
- The "$3,000 vs $30,000/month" and "past 8 brands did $1M in year one" figures are instructor claims about their own results, not independently verified benchmarks — treat as motivational framing, not a guaranteed outcome.
- As with the niche/logo/name, the why is explicitly framed as revisable, not permanent — avoid perfectionism/analysis paralysis here too.
