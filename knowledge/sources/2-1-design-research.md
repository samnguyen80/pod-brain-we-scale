# 2.1 Design Research

Stage: 2) Design
Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hduC-C8pBpE&t=3104s
Transcript: [../../transcripts/2-1-design-research.md](../../transcripts/2-1-design-research.md)

## The job of this lesson
A merchant should walk away able to do market-driven design research before touching any design tool: recognize what makes a POD design "good" vs "bad" visually, pull 10-15 real winning designs from Etsy as inspiration (never copying), and feed that research into the WeScale Design & Research GPT to get niche demographics, psychographics, and design direction.

## Playbook
1. Research first, always: skipping it means guessing, over-complicating designs, and burning time without finding winners; research-first means designing products (not art) and finding winners faster. `[00:52:23]`
2. Study proven-good design patterns: heavy negative space (ink sparing, bare fabric shows through as part of the graphic), 1-3 colors max, clean readable typography, one clear visual hierarchy (text-only, text+graphic, or graphic-only — don't mix). `[00:53:31]`
3. Learn bad-design patterns to avoid: solid ink blocks, hard-to-read cursive/gradient fonts, paragraph-length text, pixelated art, graphics bleeding into the neckline, designs sitting too low, too many colors, visible "AI slop." Download the free "good vs bad design" PDF from the WeScale community as a reference. `[00:56:16]`, `[00:58:24]`
4. Research the niche on Etsy: search "[niche] t-shirts," filter to "Etsy's Picks" / "Star Seller," and screenshot 10-15 currently-selling winners, noting demand signals (cart counts, "bought in last 24h," bestseller tags). Never copy designs directly — screenshots are for pattern inspiration only, to avoid copyright/trademark infringement. `[00:59:21]`
5. Save all screenshots into an "Etsy inspiration designs" folder inside the brand's master folder. `[01:04:23]`
6. Extract recurring patterns from the saved set: text-based/typography, clean fonts, one clear message, an emotional hook (funny/serious/sad/happy) plus a statement, clear visual hierarchy. `[01:04:52]`
7. Reframe the job: you're translating what the market already says (words, jokes, motifs) with your own spin — not inventing art from a blank canvas. `[01:05:55]`
8. Upload the screenshots to the WeScale Design & Research GPT: "analyze these designs for my [niche] print-on-demand t-shirt brand and give me a demographic breakdown, I want to know who my customers are." It returns design themes, customer segments with age/gender splits, psychographics, trending slogans/motifs, and cross-niche combos. `[01:06:23]`
9. Follow up with "give me more design direction for my [niche] brand" for proposed brand-positioning lanes/subcultures, typography and color direction, product strategy, and mood-board inspiration. `[01:10:24]`
10. Save all GPT output into a Google Doc inside the brand's folder as the reference doc for the design phase. `[01:11:25]`
11. Time-box with the "80/20 action plan": 60-minute timer, identify what 20+ researched designs have in common, write down 3 patterns, stop researching, start creating. `[01:12:15]`

## Numbers and thresholds
- Meg's own design did ~$19,519.17 in sales; WeScale member Marlin generated $20,714 from a single design `[00:51:48]` (claimed)
- Etsy demand examples logged during research: 12 carts on one design `[00:59:50]`; 5 sales/24h ≈ 35/week `[01:00:44]`; 14 carts + Etsy Pick `[01:01:13]`; 27 sales/24h ≈ 150+/week `[01:01:42]`; 6 sales/24h ≈ 42/week `[01:02:09]`; 4 sales/24h ≈ 28/week `[01:02:36]`
- Screenshot target: 10-15 designs for the inspiration bank `[01:00:17]`; later framed as 30-50 winning designs analyzed per niche `[01:06:23]`
- Cap research time at 2 hours (diminishing returns) and don't save 100+ designs (analysis paralysis) `[01:11:50]`
- 80/20 plan: 60-minute timer, 3 patterns written down `[01:12:15]`

## Tools and services named
- Etsy — marketplace used for design-inspiration research, filtered to Etsy's Picks / Star Seller `[00:59:21]`
- WeScale Design & Research GPT — custom GPT that analyzes uploaded designs and generates niche demographic/psychographic breakdowns and design direction `[00:52:55]`
- Free WeScale community — source of the "good vs bad design" PDF guide `[00:58:24]`
- Google Docs — stores the GPT's research output `[01:11:25]`

## Watch-outs
- Never copy/paste other sellers' designs onto your own products — copyright and trademark infringement risk. `[00:59:21]`
- Don't research more than 2 hours or save 100+ reference designs — both lead to diminishing returns / analysis paralysis. `[01:11:50]`
- Don't skip research and guess, and don't design for personal taste instead of the market. `[01:11:50]`
- Bad-design red flags: solid ink blocks, unreadable fonts/paragraphs, pixelated art, neckline bleed, designs sitting too low, too many colors, "AI slop" look. `[00:56:16]`
- Dollar figures cited (per-design sales) are self-reported marketing claims, not independently verified.
