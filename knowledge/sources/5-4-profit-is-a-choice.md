# 5.4 Profit is a Choice

Stage: 5) Scaling
Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hduC-C8pBpE&t=38962s
Transcript: [../../transcripts/5-4-profit-is-a-choice.md](../../transcripts/5-4-profit-is-a-choice.md)

## The job of this lesson
A merchant should be able to build and run a profit & loss statement from scratch — pulling every line from Shopify, Printify, the ad account, and overhead — on at least a weekly (ideally daily) cadence, so profitability becomes a tracked number instead of a guess.

## Playbook
1. **Mindset framing:** profitability is a decision the owner commits to, not a byproduct of luck or niche — running the P&L is described as non-negotiable `[10:49:26]`–`[10:53:30]`.
2. **What a P&L is:** income minus expenses equals net profit; run it on an accrual basis so monthly bills (e.g. a Shopify charge landing on one day) get spread evenly across days rather than distorting a single day's number `[10:53:30]`–`[10:53:58]`, `[11:05:44]`–`[11:06:39]`.
3. **Build it top to bottom**, using this order and these sources:
   1. Shopify gross sales — Analytics → date range → "Total sales over time" `[11:00:29]`.
   2. + Shipping revenue collected at checkout `[11:00:29]`.
   3. − Discounts (Shopify dashboard) `[11:01:10]`.
   4. − Refunds (Shopify dashboard) `[11:01:10]`.
   5. − Cost of goods (Printify) — Printify → Wallet → Invoices → select month → sum the relevant country's invoices (US only until scaling internationally past $10K); each invoice breaks into production cost + shipping cost + tax `[11:01:44]`–`[11:03:43]`. For a daily P&L, pull Printify's daily order export instead of the monthly invoice `[11:03:43]`–`[11:04:08]`.
   6. − Transaction fees — Shopify → Finance → Payouts (or estimate flat ~4-5%) `[11:04:08]`–`[11:05:07]`.
   7. − Facebook ad spend, pulled directly from the ad account for the date range; Google Ads is skipped entirely until $30-50K/month `[10:57:30]`–`[10:58:01]`, `[11:05:07]`.
   8. − Overhead — list every subscription/app on its own tab; convert to a daily rate for a daily P&L (e.g. $90/month → $3/day) `[11:05:44]`–`[11:06:11]`.
   9. − Contractors / customer-service help / bookkeeper, once relevant `[10:58:01]`–`[10:58:27]`.
   10. + Add back any rebates: Printify volume rebate (tiered — a percentage back on cost of goods once volume qualifies) and business-credit-card cashback `[10:58:57]`–`[10:59:24]`.
4. **Worked example (November, case-study brand):** gross profit $157,000; transaction fees $12,000; Facebook spend $17,000 (Google negligible); total operating income $32,000; plus Printify rebate and card cashback → grand total income $44,000 at a 16% net margin `[10:56:58]`–`[10:59:24]`.

## Numbers and thresholds
- Prior-state margin before running a P&L: 3-5% at low volume; after adopting the P&L discipline: 20%+ `[10:49:56]`–`[10:50:26]` (claimed)
- Cost of goods (Printify) target: 40-42% of revenue, max — above 44-45% is called "tough" `[10:53:58]`, `[10:54:31]`
- Ad spend target: 25-33% of revenue (implies an overall ROAS around 3x) `[10:54:31]`
- Overhead target: ~3% of revenue `[10:54:31]`–`[10:55:00]`; one cautionary example let this creep to ~10% (~$35,000/month in software) versus a proper ~$5,000/month even on top-tier Shopify + Klaviyo plans `[10:55:00]`–`[10:55:28]` (claimed)
- Refund rate benchmark: ~1% `[10:56:58]`
- Transaction fee rate example: ~5.7% for this brand (runs international); typical US-only range ~4-5% `[11:04:36]`–`[11:05:07]`
- November worked example: gross profit $157,000; transaction fees $12,000; Facebook spend $17,000; total operating income $32,000; grand total income $44,000 at 16% margin `[10:56:58]`–`[10:59:24]` (claimed)
- Reconciliation note: the brand's own P&L (gross sales reported as $304,000) and Triple Whale's dashboard (reported as $283,000, which excludes discounts/refunds) are not cleanly reconciled on camera — first described as "off by ... less than 1%," then re-estimated as "off by about 10-12%" `[10:55:56]`–`[11:00:02]`. Treat the exact gross-sales figure as ambiguous; the $44,000/16% net profit outcome is the number to trust.
- The Printify-rebate/cashback add-back line is also internally inconsistent in the source ("$4,600 in profit" vs. immediately after "additional income of $1,582" for what appears to be the same line) `[10:59:24]` — flagged rather than resolved; don't cite either figure as precise.
- Bookkeeper/accountant recommended once revenue reaches ~$100,000/month `[10:58:01]`–`[10:58:27]`
- Google Ads not worth exploring below $30-50K/month `[10:58:01]`

## Decision rules
- If you haven't hit the point where Shopify prompts you to collect sales tax in a given state → don't collect it yet; wait for Shopify's notice `[11:01:10]`–`[11:01:44]`.
- If monthly revenue is below $30-50K → skip Google Ads entirely.
- If monthly revenue is below ~$100K → skip hiring a bookkeeper/accountant; handle it yourself with disciplined tracking.
- Always keep business and personal expenses on separate cards — makes P&L tracking and compliance dramatically simpler `[10:58:27]`–`[10:58:57]`.
- Run the P&L at minimum weekly; run it daily if aiming for the fastest feedback loop `[10:53:30]`.

## Tools and services named
- Printify — Wallet → Invoices (cost of goods source) and volume-based rebate tiers (~5% back on cost of goods at this brand's scale)
- Shopify — Analytics (gross sales, discounts, refunds) and Finance → Payouts (transaction fees)
- Triple Whale — free tool connecting Shopify + Klaviyo + Facebook for a blended metrics dashboard; used here as a cross-check against the manual P&L (with an unreconciled gap, see above)
- Capital One business credit card — 2% cash back on all business spend, added back as a P&L income line
- ChatGPT — referenced in passing for researching state sales-tax rules

## Watch-outs
- Overhead creep is easy to miss — the instructor's own software spend reached ~10% of revenue before being caught `[10:55:00]`.
- The source's own worked numbers contain two unreconciled discrepancies (gross-sales figure vs. Triple Whale, and the cashback add-back line) — verify independently rather than treating either as exact `[10:55:56]`–`[11:00:02]`, `[10:59:24]`.
- Don't expect to know every number to "expert level" — the instructor explicitly says getting comfortable with some uncertainty is fine as long as the P&L is run consistently `[11:07:02]`.
