# 1.2 Create Your Brand

Stage: 1) Branding
Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hduC-C8pBpE&t=1231s
Transcript: [../../transcripts/1-2-create-your-brand.md](../../transcripts/1-2-create-your-brand.md)

## The job of this lesson
A merchant should walk away with a validated niche, a brand name, and a cleaned-up logo (main + favicon + side-by-side versions) — produced in under an hour using a custom GPT plus Canva — and should internalize that customers buy community/belonging, not just product quality.

## Playbook
1. Reframe what's actually being sold: reviews of successful brands (e.g. Sloth Hiking Club) show customers buy the sense of community/identity the brand speaks to, not just shirt quality — design brand messaging around one clear customer and one product type, not a broad catalog. `[00:21:01]`
2. Open the "POD Brand Builder GPT" (free, via the WeScale School community → Classroom → course → linked GPT below the lesson). `[00:23:31]`
3. Give the GPT exactly one input: a stream-of-consciousness list of your own genuine hobbies/interests/passions/topics — this is the only input needed, and it must be something you're personally interested in so the brand stays sustainable to work on long-term. `[00:24:21]`
4. Let the GPT validate the niche (market size check) and propose several sub-angles/genres combining your interests; pick the angle you personally feel most connected to, explicitly avoiding anything involving guns/drugs/alcohol as a niche theme. `[00:26:05]`
5. Ask the GPT for brand name options (~20 candidates); target 2-3 words max, simple, and evocative of the brand vibe (not just literal, e.g. "Sloth Hiking Club" implies a whole tone, not just hiking). Give the GPT feedback to refine (e.g. "I like wordplay on X, keep it simple") and let it re-generate; take its final top recommendation. Limit this step to ~5 minutes. `[00:28:13]`
6. Ask the GPT to generate a logo for the chosen name; iterate feedback only 1-2 times (e.g. "make it less traditional, more fun/humor") since the logo can always change later. Check the result against 3 criteria: not too many colors, not too busy/cluttered, and instantly communicates the brand name/vibe at a glance. `[00:29:57]`
7. Download the logo and open Canva (free tier ok) to polish it: create a custom 1080x1080 canvas, drag in the logo, remove the background, and generate a white-background version (most placements use a plain/white background, per the Sloth Hiking Club reference). `[00:32:28]`
8. Clean up any stray artifacts left by the AI-generated logo image. `[00:33:31]`
9. Crop a standalone icon/mark from the logo to use as the favicon (the small browser-tab icon) — pick a piece of the design that reads well on its own. `[00:34:34]`
10. Create a side-by-side (horizontal) logo lockup variant for placements where a tall/stacked logo doesn't fit — crop icon and text, position side by side, resize to match proportion. `[00:35:26]`
11. Export/download all 3 versions (main, side-by-side, favicon) with transparent backgrounds, clearly named (e.g. "Slice Club main logo," "Slice Club favicon"), into one organized desktop folder. `[00:36:11]`
12. Move on immediately rather than perfecting the name/logo/niche — treat all three as changeable, and prioritize forward progress over certainty.

## Numbers and thresholds
- Time budget for naming: only ~5 minutes on the brand-name back-and-forth `[00:29:19]`
- Recommended logo iteration limit: 1-2 feedback rounds max `[00:30:33]`
- Estimated cost avoided by using this GPT + Canva flow vs. hiring out: $500–$2,000 in setup cost `[00:36:44]` (claimed)
- Time avoided vs. old manual process: 2-4 weeks `[00:37:21]` (claimed)
- Niche validation example stat cited by the GPT: 90% of golfers self-report as "not good," used to justify the "bad golfer" self-deprecating humor niche as broadly relatable `[00:27:39]` (claimed, tool-generated)
- Canva canvas size used for logo cleanup: 1080x1080 `[00:32:28]`

## Tools and services named
- POD Brand Builder GPT (custom ChatGPT-based tool, free via WeScale School community) — generates validated niche, brand name options, and logo
- Canva — used to remove logo background, generate favicon and side-by-side lockup, export transparent PNGs — free tier sufficient for this step
- WeScale School community (free tier) — where the GPT and other course resources are linked per-lesson

## Watch-outs
- Avoid "fallacy of the perfect pick": don't over-optimize name/logo/niche upfront — the instructor explicitly recounts overpaying a Latin translator for a brand name in the past as a cautionary example of wasted effort/money. `[00:37:21]`
- Explicitly avoid niches touching guns, drugs, or alcohol. `[00:26:35]`
- Don't pick a niche you're not personally interested in, even if research says it's a good market — the instructor states this made a past brand unsustainable to work on long-term. `[00:24:48]`
- Anecdotes about Disney, Amazon (originally "Cadabra"), and Google (originally "BackRub") having bad original names are used to argue naming imperfection is normal — treat as illustrative, not verified corporate history claims. `[00:38:38]`
